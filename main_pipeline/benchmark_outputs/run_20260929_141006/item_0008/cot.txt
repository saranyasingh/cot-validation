EXPLANATION
1. Initially, Carla downloads at a rate of 2 GB per minute.
2. She needs to download a total of 200 GB.
3. At first, she continues at this speed until 40% of the total time (i.e., \( \frac{2}{5} \) or 40 minutes) have passed.
4. During these 40 minutes, she downloads \( 2 \times 40 = 80 \) GB.
5. After the restart due to Windows forcing a restart, Carla starts over at her initial speed of 2 GB per minute.
6. To catch up, she must cover the remaining distance between where she left off before the restart and the end of the file.
7. This additional distance is \( 200 - 80 = 120 \) GB.
8. Since she now only has 16 minutes available (after the restart), we need to determine how many more GB she can download in those 16 minutes.
9. In 16 minutes, Carla can download \( 2 \times 16 = 32 \) GB.

Therefore, Carla's new effective download speed is 32 GB per minute.

To find out how long it will take her to download the entire 200 GB file after restarting, we divide the remaining distance by her new speed:
\[ \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Remaining Distance}}{\text{New Speed}} = \frac{120 \text{ GB}}{32 \text{ GB/min}} = 3.75 \text{ minutes}. \]

Adding the time taken during the initial period to the time taken after restarting gives us the total time required:
\[ \text{Total Time} = 40 \text{ min} + 3.75 \text{ min} = 43.75 \text{ minutes}. \]

The answer is: 43.75
