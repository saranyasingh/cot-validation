To determine how much Kylar needs to pay for 16 glasses, we first identify the cost structure of the glasses.

1. **Identify the base price**: Each glass initially costs $5.
2. **Determine the discount rate**: Every second glass gets a 60% discount on its original price. This means that if a glass has an odd position (e.g., 1st or 3rd), it retains its full price; if it has an even position (e.g., 2nd or 4th), it is discounted to 60% of its original price.

Given this pattern, we can calculate the total cost as follows:

- For odd-numbered glasses, they retain their full price of $5.
- For even-numbered glasses, they receive a 60% discount, so their price becomes \(5 \times 0.6 = \$3\).

Now, let's count the number of odd and even numbered glasses among the first 16 glasses:

- Odd-numbered glasses: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 → 8 glasses
- Even-numbered glasses: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 → 8 glasses

Next, we calculate the total cost for each type of glass separately:

- Cost for odd-numbered glasses: \(8 \text{ glasses} \times \$5/\text{glass} = \$40\)
- Cost for even-numbered glasses: \(8 \text{ glasses} \times \$3/\text{glass} = \$24\)

Finally, we add these two amounts together to get the total cost:

\[ \$40 + \$24 = \$64 \]

Thus, Kylar needs to pay a total of $\boxed{64}$ dollars for the 16 glasses.
