To solve this problem, we need to establish relationships between the number of sheep in Toulouse, Charleston, and Seattle.

**Premise 1:** Seattle has 20 sheep.
\[ \text{Seattle} = 20 \]

**Premise 2:** Charleston has 4 times as many sheep as Seattle.
\[ \text{Charleston} = 4 \times \text{Seattle} = 4 \times 20 \]
\[ \text{Charleston} = 80 \]

**Premise 3:** Toulouse has twice as many sheep as Charleston.
\[ \text{Toulouse} = 2 \times \text{Charleston} = 2 \times 80 \]
\[ \text{Toulouse} = 160 \]

Now that we know how many sheep each city has individually:
- Seattle: 20 sheep
- Charleston: 80 sheep
- Toulouse: 160 sheep

To find out how many sheep they all have together:
\[ \text{Total Sheep} = \text{Seattle} + \text{Charleston} + \text{Toulouse} \]
\[ \text{Total Sheep} = 20 + 80 + 160 \]
\[ \text{Total Sheep} = 260 \]

Therefore, the total number of sheep in Toulouse, Charleston, and Seattle is **260**.
