Explanation:

Let's break down the problem into two parts to form our logical syllogism.

**Premise 1:** Gretchen has 110 coins.
- This is the total number of coins Gretchen possesses.

**Premise 2:** There are 30 more gold coins than silver coins.
- This statement gives us an additional relationship between the number of gold and silver coins.

To find out how many gold coins Gretchen has, we need to determine the number of silver coins first. Let's denote the number of silver coins as S.

From Premise 2, we know that there are 30 more gold coins than silver coins. Therefore, if we let G represent the number of gold coins, we can write this relationship as:
\[ G = S + 30 \]

Now, since the total number of coins (G + S) is given as 110, we can set up the following equation:
\[ G + S = 110 \]

Substituting the expression for G from Premise 2 into this equation, we get:
\[ (S + 30) + S = 110 \]
\[ 2S + 30 = 110 \]

Next, we solve for S:
\[ 2S = 110 - 30 \]
\[ 2S = 80 \]
\[ S = 40 \]

So, Gretchen has 40 silver coins.

Finally, using the relationship \( G = S + 30 \), we substitute the value of S back into the equation:
\[ G = 40 + 30 \]
\[ G = 70 \]

Therefore, Gretchen has 70 gold coins.

The answer is: 70
