EXPLANATION

1. Let's denote the price of a blue tie as \( B \).
   - Given: \( B = \$40 \).

2. John buys twice as many red ties as blue ties.
   - Therefore, if he buys \( x \) blue ties, he buys \( 2x \) red ties.

3. Red ties cost 50% more than blue ties.
   - Thus, the price of a red tie is \( B + 0.5B = 1.5B \).
   - Substituting \( B = \$40 \):
     \[
     \text{Price of a red tie} = 1.5 \times 40 = \$60
     \]

4. John spends $200 on blue ties at $40 each.
   - Number of blue ties bought:
     \[
     \frac{\$200}{\$40} = 5 \text{ blue ties}
     \]

5. Since John buys twice as many red ties as blue ties,
   - Number of red ties bought:
     \[
     2 \times 5 = 10 \text{ red ties}
     \]

6. Total amount spent on blue ties:
   \[
   5 \times 40 = \$200
   \]

7. Total amount spent on red ties:
   \[
   10 \times 60 = \$600
   \]

8. Total expenditure on all ties:
   \[
   200 + 600 = \$800
   \]

Therefore, the total amount John spent on ties is **800** dollars.
