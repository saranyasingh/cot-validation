To solve this problem, we need to follow these steps:

**Premise 1:** Darrell and Allen's ages are in the ratio of 7:11.

Let's denote Darrell's age as \( D \) and Allen's age as \( A \).

According to the given ratio,
\[ \frac{D}{A} = \frac{7}{11} \]

This means that for every 7 parts of Darrell's age, Allen's age has 11 parts. Therefore, if we let \( x \) represent the common multiple (the product of the ratios), then:
\[ D = 7x \]
\[ A = 11x \]

**Premise 2:** Their total age now is 162.

So, we have:
\[ D + A = 162 \]

Substituting \( D \) and \( A \) from Premise 1 into this equation gives us:
\[ 7x + 11x = 162 \]

Combining like terms, we get:
\[ 18x = 162 \]

Solving for \( x \):
\[ x = \frac{162}{18} \]
\[ x = 9 \]

Now, using the value of \( x \) to find the current ages of both Darrell and Allen:
\[ D = 7x = 7 \times 9 = 63 \]
\[ A = 11x = 11 \times 9 = 99 \]

Therefore, currently, Darrell is 63 years old and Allen is 99 years old.

**Premise 3:** Calculate Allen's age 10 years from now.

In 10 years, Allen’s age will be:
\[ A_{\text{future}} = A + 10 \]
\[ A_{\text{future}} = 99 + 10 \]
\[ A_{\text{future}} = 109 \]

Thus, Allen's age 10 years from now will be **109**.
