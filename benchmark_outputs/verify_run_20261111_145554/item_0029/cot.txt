To determine how many miles Henry traveled between his first and second stops, we need to follow these steps:

**Premise 1:** Henry's total trip distance is 60 miles.

**Premise 2:** His first stop occurs at 20 miles into his journey.

**Premise 3:** His second stop is 15 miles before the end of the trip.

Firstly, let's find out where his second stop is located on the trip:

- Total distance = 60 miles.
- First stop location = 20 miles.

Now, subtract the first stop location from the total distance to find the remaining distance until the second stop:

\[ \text{Remaining distance} = \text{Total distance} - \text{First stop location} \]
\[ \text{Remaining distance} = 60 \text{ miles} - 20 \text{ miles} \]
\[ \text{Remaining distance} = 40 \text{ miles} \]

This means that Henry has 40 miles left after his first stop but before his second stop. However, it states that his second stop is 15 miles before the end of the trip. Therefore, the actual distance between his first and second stops must include both the remaining 40 miles and the additional 15 miles:

\[ \text{Distance between stops} = \text{Remaining distance} + \text{Distance from last mile marker} \]
\[ \text{Distance between stops} = 40 \text{ miles} + 15 \text{ miles} \]
\[ \text{Distance between stops} = 55 \text{ miles} \]

Thus, Henry traveled **55 miles** between his first and second stops.

The answer is: 55
