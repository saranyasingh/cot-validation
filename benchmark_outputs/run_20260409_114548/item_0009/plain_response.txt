To determine how far John is from home after driving for 4 hours, we need to calculate his total distance traveled and subtract it from the distance he would have been if he had not turned back.

1. **Distance driven initially (first 3 hours):**
   \[
   \text{Speed} = 60 \text{ mph}, \quad \text{Time} = 3 \text{ hours}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Distance} = 60 \times 3 = 180 \text{ miles}
   \]

2. **Total distance without turning back:**
   If John did not turn back, he would travel another 180 miles:
   \[
   \text{Total Distance Without Turning Back} = 180 + 180 = 360 \text{ miles}
   \]

3. **Distance spent in standstill traffic:**
   \[
   \text{Speed} = 0 \text{ mph}, \quad \text{Time} = 2 \text{ hours}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Distance} = 0 \text{ miles}
   \]

4. **Remaining distance to be covered:**
   Subtract the distances already accounted for from the initial planned trip distance:
   \[
   \text{Remaining Distance} = 360 - 180 = 180 \text{ miles}
   \]

5. **Distance driven during the last part of the journey:**
   John drives at 80 mph for the remaining 2 hours.
   \[
   \text{Distance} = 80 \times 2 = 160 \text{ miles}
   \]

6. **Final distance from home:**
   Add the remaining distance to the last part of the journey to find out how far John is from home:
   \[
   \text{Final Distance From Home} = 180 + 160 = 340 \text{ miles}
   \]

Therefore, John is 340 miles away from home at the end of those 4 hours. The answer is: 340.
