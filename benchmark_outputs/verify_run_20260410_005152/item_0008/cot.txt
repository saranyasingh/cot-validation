EXPLANATION
1. First, we need to calculate how much of the file Carla has downloaded before the restart.
   - The total size of the file is 200 GB.
   - She downloads at a rate of 2 GB per minute.
   - At this speed, it would normally take her \( \frac{200}{2} = 100 \) minutes to finish the entire download.

2. Next, let's determine how long it took for Carla to complete the first half of the download before the restart.
   - Since she completed 50 GB in the first half (half of 200 GB), it would have taken her \( \frac{50}{2} = 25 \) minutes to download that part.

3. Now, we know that during these 25 minutes, there was a 20-minute window when Windows forced a restart. This means the actual time spent downloading slowed down significantly due to the restart.
   
4. To find out the effective download rate after the restart, we divide the remaining time by the reduced download capacity.
   - Remaining time after the restart = Total time minus time before restart = 100 minutes - 25 minutes = 75 minutes.
   - Reduced download capacity = Initial rate * Time before restart / New time = 2 GB/min * 25 min / 75 min = 10 GB/min.

5. Finally, we add back the time before the restart to get the total time required to download the whole file.
   - Total time = Time before restart + Restart time = 25 min + 20 min = 45 minutes.

Therefore, the answer is: 45
