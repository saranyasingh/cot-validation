To determine how much Janet makes every day at the farmers' market, we need to follow these steps:

**Premise 1:** Janet's ducks lay 16 eggs per day.

This establishes the total number of eggs laid daily by her ducks.

**Premise 2:** Janet eats three eggs for breakfast every morning.

By eating three eggs out of the 16 laid daily, we reduce the number of eggs available for other uses or sale.

**Premise 3:** Janet bakes muffins for her friends every day using four eggs.

After accounting for breakfast consumption (three eggs), there are \(16 - 3 = 13\) eggs remaining that can be used for baking muffins.

**Premise 4:** Janet sells the remainder at the farmers' market daily for $2 per fresh duck egg.

Since there are 13 eggs left after making muffins and selling them back into the market, and each egg is sold for $2, we calculate the earnings as follows:

\[
\text{Earnings} = \text{Number of Eggs Sold} \times \text{Price per Egg}
\]

Substituting the values:

\[
\text{Earnings} = 13 \times \$2 = \$26
\]

Therefore, Janet makes \$26 every day at the farmers' market.

**The answer is: 26**

In summary, through careful deduction based on the given information about egg production and sales, we determined that Janet earns \$26 every day at the farmers' market.
