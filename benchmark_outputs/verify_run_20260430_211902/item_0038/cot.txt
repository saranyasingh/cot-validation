EXPLANATION

1. John starts with 13 Lego sets.
2. Each Lego set is sold for $15.
   - Total revenue from selling Lego sets = \( 13 \times 15 = \$195 \)
3. John uses this total revenue ($195) to buy 8 video games at $20 each.
4. Total cost of buying 8 video games = \( 8 \times 20 = \$160 \)
5. After purchasing the video games, John has $5 left.

Now let's calculate how much money John spent on the video games:

\[ \text{Money spent} = \text{Total revenue} - \text{Remaining money} \]
\[ \text{Money spent} = 195 - 5 = \$190 \]

This means John used all his revenue from selling Lego sets to purchase the video games, leaving him with $5 as remaining money. Since he only had $190 available after buying the video games, we can deduce that before buying the video games, he must have had more than $190 but less than or equal to $205 (since he could not spend exactly $190).

Since he started with 13 Lego sets, subtracting the amount spent on video games gives us the remaining Lego sets:

\[ \text{Remaining Lego sets} = 13 - (\text{Money spent on video games}) \]
\[ \text{Remaining Lego sets} = 13 - 160 \] (which would imply negative value)

Clearly, it doesn't make sense to subtract more than the initial inventory since we're dealing with physical items. Therefore, the calculation above shows an inconsistency due to misinterpretation of spending limits.

Revisiting our steps, the correct interpretation should consider the actual financial transactions accurately:

- Initial sale revenue: $195
- Cost of 8 video games: $160
- Remaining money: $5

Given these figures, if we correctly account for the expenditure on video games within the constraints provided:

\[ \text{Initial Lego sets} = 13 \]
\[ \text{Cost of video games} = 160 \]
\[ \text{Remaining money} = 5 \]

To find the number of Lego sets left, we need to adjust for the fact that the total revenue was $195 and he ended up with $5 after expenditures:

\[ \text{Legos bought} = 8 \]
\[ \text{Revenue per Lego set} = 15 \]
\[ \text{Lego sets sold} = 13 \]
\[ \text{Total revenue} = 13 \times 15 = 195 \]

After accounting for expenses on video games:

\[ \text{Spent on video games} = 160 \]
\[ \text{Remaining money} = 5 \]

So, initially having 13 Lego sets, we now have:

\[ \text{Remaining Lego sets} = 13 - 8 = 5 \]

Therefore, John has 5 Lego sets left after selling them and using some of the proceeds to buy video games.
