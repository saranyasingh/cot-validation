EXPLANATION
Let's break down the information given and use it to form our logical argument.

1. **Premise 1**: Every day, Wendi feeds each of her chickens three cups of mixed chicken feed.
   - This tells us that regardless of the time or the amount given earlier, each chicken gets 3 cups per feeding.

2. **Premise 2**: She gives the chickens their feed in three separate meals.
   - This means that the total feed intake over the course of the day consists of these three separate servings.

3. **Premise 3**: In the morning, she gives her flock of chickens 15 cups of feed.
   - We know this because we're told specifically about the morning feed distribution.

4. **Premise 4**: In the afternoon, she gives her chickens another 25 cups of feed.
   - Similarly, this is mentioned separately as part of the afternoon feed distribution.

Now let's combine all this information into a logical syllogism.

**Conclusion**: To find out how many cups of feed Wendi needs to give her chickens in the final meal of the day, we first determine the total amount of feed distributed so far and then subtract this from the total daily requirement (which is twice the total fed throughout the day).

- Total feed given in the morning = 15 cups
- Total feed given in the afternoon = 25 cups
- Total feed given between morning and afternoon = 15 + 25 = 40 cups

Since there are 20 chickens, the total daily requirement for all chickens combined would be:
\[ \text{Total daily requirement} = 40 \times 20 = 800 \text{ cups} \]

Each chicken receives 3 cups per meal, so for 20 chickens:
\[ \text{Daily requirement per chicken} = 3 \times 20 = 60 \text{ cups/day} \]
This means the total daily requirement for all chickens combined is still 800 cups.

Given that 40 cups have already been distributed, the remaining feed needed for the final meal is:
\[ \text{Remaining feed needed} = 800 - 40 = 760 \text{ cups} \]

Therefore, Wendi needs to give her chickens 760 cups of feed in the final meal of the day.

The answer is: 760
