EXPLANATION
Let's break down the problem into steps using a logical syllogism.

1. Let \( B \) represent the price of the boots.
2. According to the problem, one pair of heels costs $33.
3. The other pair of heels costs twice as much as the first pair, so it costs \( 2 \times 33 = \$66 \).
4. Together, the two pairs of heels cost $5 more than the boots (\$66 + \$33 = \$99).
5. Therefore, if we let \( H_1 \) represent the first pair of heels at $33, and \( H_2 \) represents the second pair of heels at $66, their total cost is \( H_1 + H_2 = 99 \).

Now, since the total cost of both pairs of heels plus the boots equals the cost of the boots alone (because the boots cost more), we have:
\[ B = H_1 + H_2 = 99 \]

Thus, the boots cost $99.

The answer is: 99
