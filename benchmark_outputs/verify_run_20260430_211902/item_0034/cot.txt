To solve this problem, we need to set up an equation based on the information given.

Let's denote:
- \( G \) as the number of gold coins.
- \( S \) as the number of silver coins.

From the problem statement, we know two things:
1. Gretchen has a total of 110 coins.
2. There are 30 more gold coins than silver coins.

We can express these facts mathematically:
1. \( G + S = 110 \)
2. \( G = S + 30 \)

Now, let's use substitution to find the values of \( G \) and \( S \).

First, substitute the expression for \( G \) from the second equation into the first equation:
\[ (S + 30) + S = 110 \]

Next, simplify and solve for \( S \):
\[ 2S + 30 = 110 \]
Subtract 30 from both sides:
\[ 2S = 80 \]
Divide by 2:
\[ S = 40 \]

So, Gretchen has 40 silver coins. Now, using the second equation to find the number of gold coins:
\[ G = S + 30 \]
\[ G = 40 + 30 \]
\[ G = 70 \]

Therefore, Gretchen has 70 gold coins.

The answer is: 70
