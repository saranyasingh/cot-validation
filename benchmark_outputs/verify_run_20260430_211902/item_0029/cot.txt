To determine how many miles Henry traveled between his first and second stops, we need to follow these steps:

**Premise 1:** Henry's total distance traveled on the bike trip is 60 miles.
- This establishes the total distance as a known fact.

**Premise 2:** Henry's first stop occurs at 20 miles into his journey.
- This specifies the location of his first stop.

**Premise 3:** His second stop is 15 miles before the end of the trip.
- This indicates the position of his second stop relative to the start of the trip.

Let's break down the information provided:

1. **Total Distance (D):**
   - \( D = 60 \) miles

2. **First Stop Location (F):**
   - \( F = 20 \) miles

3. **Second Stop Location (S):**
   - Since it is stated that "his second stop is 15 miles before the end of the trip," this means:
     - \( S = D - 15 \)
     - Substituting \( D = 60 \):
       - \( S = 60 - 15 \)
       - \( S = 45 \) miles

Now, we need to find out how far he traveled between the first and second stops.

**Calculation:**
- Distance between the first and second stops (\( L \)):
  - \( L = S - F \)
  - Substituting the values:
    - \( L = 45 - 20 \)
    - \( L = 25 \) miles

Therefore, Henry traveled 25 miles between his first and second stops.

The answer is: 25
