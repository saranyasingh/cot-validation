Let's denote the number of apples Jackie has as \( J \).

According to the problem:
1. Adam has 4 more apples than Jackie.
   So, Adam has \( J + 4 \) apples.
2. Together, Adam and Jackie have 14 apples.
   Therefore, \( (J + 4) + J = 14 \).
3. Solving for \( J \):
   \[
   2J + 4 = 14 \\
   2J = 10 \\
   J = 5
   \]
   Thus, Jackie has 5 apples.

Now we can find out how many apples Adam has:
   Adam has \( J + 4 = 5 + 4 = 9 \) apples.

Finally, we calculate how many apples Bob has, who has 6 apples more than Adam and Jackie together:
   Bob has \( 9 + 5 + 6 = 20 \) apples.

So, Bob has 20 apples. The answer is: 20
