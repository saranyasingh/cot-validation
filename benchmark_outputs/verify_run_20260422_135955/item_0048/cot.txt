EXPLANATION
1. Let's denote the price of a blue tie as B dollars.
2. According to the problem, John bought blue ties at $40 each, so we have B = $40.
3. Since John buys twice as many red ties as blue ties, let's say he bought R red ties instead of B blue ties.
4. Given that the red ties cost 50% more than the blue ties, we calculate the price of a red tie (R) as follows: R = B + 0.5B = 1.5 * B.
5. Substituting the value of B into this equation gives us R = 1.5 * $40 = $60 per red tie.
6. Now, let's consider the total amount John spent on blue ties. He bought these at $40 each, totaling $200.
7. If he had bought blue ties, his expenditure would have been $200.
8. For red ties, since they cost 50% more, we need to find out how many red ties he could buy with the same budget ($200).
9. To do this, we divide the total amount spent on red ties by their individual price: (Red Ties Cost / Red Tie Price) = $200 / $60 = 3.333... ≈ 3.33.
10. Therefore, John could buy approximately 3.33 red ties with the $200 budget, but since he cannot purchase a fraction of a tie, we round down to 3 red ties.
11. The total cost for the red ties is then calculated as: 3 * $60 = $180.

CONCLUSION
In summary, after calculating all costs associated with both blue and red ties, John spent $180 on ties.
