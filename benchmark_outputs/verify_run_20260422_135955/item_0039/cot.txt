Explanation:
1. John runs 60 miles a week.
2. He runs 3 days a week.
3. He runs 3 hours on the first day.
4. On the other two days, he runs half as much time as the first day.

To find out how fast he runs, we need to calculate his average speed over the three days.

First, let's determine the total running time for each day:
- First day: 3 hours
- Second day: \( \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \) hours
- Third day: \( \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \) hours

Next, we sum up the total running time for all three days:
\[ 3 + 1.5 + 1.5 = 6 \text{ hours} \]

Now, we know that John ran 60 miles in 6 hours. To find his average speed (in miles per hour), we divide the total distance by the total time:
\[ \text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{60 \text{ miles}}{6 \text{ hours}} = 10 \text{ mph} \]

Therefore, John runs at an average speed of 10 miles per hour.

The answer is: 10
