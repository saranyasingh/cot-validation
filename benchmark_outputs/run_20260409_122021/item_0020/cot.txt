EXPLANATION
To determine Marissa's required walking speed on the remaining portion of the trail, we start by calculating the total time she has already spent hiking.

1. **Total Distance Traveled**: Marissa has hiked a total of \(12\) miles.
   - Time taken for the first 4 miles = 1 hour
   - Time taken for the next 2 miles = 1 hour
   - Total time so far = 1 + 1 = 2 hours

2. **Remaining Distance**: The remaining distance to hike is \(12 - 6 = 6\) miles (since 12 - 4 - 2 = 6).

3. **Desired Average Speed**: Marissa aims to achieve an average speed of 4 miles per hour over the entire trip.

Using the formula for average speed (\(\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}\)), we set up our equation as follows:

\[ \text{Desired Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} \]

Given that she wants to maintain an average speed of 4 mph over the entire trip:

\[ 4 = \frac{12}{\text{Total Time}} \]

Solving for the total time gives us:

\[ \text{Total Time} = \frac{12}{4} = 3 \text{ hours} \]

Now, we calculate how long it would take her to complete the remaining 6 miles at her desired average speed of 4 mph:

\[ \text{Time Needed} = \frac{\text{Remaining Distance}}{\text{Desired Average Speed}} = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5 \text{ hours} \]

Therefore, Marissa needs to walk at a speed of 4 miles per hour for the remaining 6 miles if she wants her average speed for the entire journey to be 4 miles per hour.
