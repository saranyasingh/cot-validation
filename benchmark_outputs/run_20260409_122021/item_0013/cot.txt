To determine how many years Carlos needs to start earning money on his lemon tree, we need to calculate the net profit per year and then see when that amount exceeds zero.

**Premise 1:** The initial investment to plant the lemon tree is $90.
**Premise 2:** Each year, the tree grows 7 lemons at a selling price of $1.5 each.
**Premise 3:** Watering and feeding the tree costs $3 annually.

Let's denote:
- \( n \) as the number of years Carlos has planted the lemon tree.

### Step-by-step analysis:

#### Yearly Profit Calculation:
- **Revenue from selling lemons:** \( 7 \text{ lemons/year} \times \$1.5/\text{lemon} = \$10.50 \text{ per year} \).
- **Costs (watering and feeding):** \( \$3 \text{ per year} \).

Net profit per year after accounting for these expenses:
\[ \text{Net Profit} = (\text{Revenue}) - (\text{Costs}) \]
\[ \text{Net Profit} = \$10.50 - \$3 = \$7.50 \]

This means that every year, Carlos makes a profit of $7.50 from the lemon tree.

#### Break-even Point Analysis:
For Carlos to start earning money, the net profit must exceed zero. We set up an equation to find out how many years it takes for the cumulative profit to equal or surpass the initial investment of $90.

Let \( P_n \) represent the total profit after \( n \) years.

\[ P_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\$7.50 - \$3) \]

Simplifying this sum:

\[ P_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \$4.50 \]

Since there are \( n \) terms in the series, each contributing $4.50, the total profit over \( n \) years is:

\[ P_n = 4.50n \]

We want to find the smallest integer \( n \) such that \( P_n > 90 \):

\[ 4.50n > 90 \]

Solving for \( n \):

\[ n > \frac{90}{4.50} \]
\[ n > 20 \]

Therefore, Carlos must wait more than 20 years to start earning money on the lemon tree.

### Conclusion:
It will take Carlos more than 20 years to break even and start making money from his lemon tree. Thus, the answer is:

**The answer is: 21**
