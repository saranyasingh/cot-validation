EXPLANATION

Let's break down the problem into smaller steps.

1. **Initial Composition of Orange Drink:**
   - We start with 10 liters of orange drink.
   - This orange drink has a ratio of two-thirds water.
   - Calculate the amount of water in the initial orange drink:
     \[
     \text{Water in orange drink} = \frac{2}{3} \times 10 = \frac{20}{3} \approx 6.67 \text{ liters}
     \]
   - Therefore, the amount of orange concentrate left after spilling 1 liter is:
     \[
     10 - 1 = 9 \text{ liters}
     \]

2. **Composition of Pineapple Drink:**
   - We have 15 liters of pineapple drink.
   - This pineapple drink has a ratio of three-fifths water.
   - Calculate the amount of water in the pineapple drink:
     \[
     \text{Water in pineapple drink} = \frac{3}{5} \times 15 = 9 \text{ liters}
     \]

3. **Total Water Before Spill:**
   - Add the amounts of water from both drinks before any spill occurred:
     \[
     \text{Total water initially} = 6.67 + 9 = 15.67 \text{ liters}
     \]

4. **Spilled Water:**
   - We spilled 1 liter of orange drink.
   - Since this was part of the orange drink mix, we need to account for the water content in the spilled portion:
     \[
     \text{Water in spilled orange drink} = \frac{2}{3} \times 1 = \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.67 \text{ liters}
     \]

5. **Remaining Water After Spill:**
   - Subtract the amount of water lost due to the spill from the total initial water:
     \[
     \text{Remaining water} = 15.67 - 0.67 = 15 \text{ liters}
     \]

Therefore, the remaining 24 liters contain approximately 15 liters of water. The answer is: 15.
