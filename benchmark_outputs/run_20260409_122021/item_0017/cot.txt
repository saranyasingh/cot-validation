EXPLANATION
To determine the total distance covered by each train over the two days, we need to calculate the distances traveled during their respective journeys.

First, let's consider the journey on the first day when the trains are traveling westward.
- Each train travels 80 miles on the first day.
- Therefore, the total distance covered by each train on the first day is 80 miles.

Next, let's look at the second day when the trains start traveling northward.
- Each train covers an additional 150 miles on the second day.
- Since there are two trains, this means that together, they cover \(2 \times 150 = 300\) miles on the second day.

Now, we add up all the distances covered by each train over the two days.
- Distance covered by each train on the first day: 80 miles
- Total distance covered by each train on the second day: 300 miles

So, the total distance covered by each train in the two days is:
\[ 80 + 300 = 380 \text{ miles} \]

Therefore, the distance covered by each train in the two days is 380 miles. The answer is: 380.
