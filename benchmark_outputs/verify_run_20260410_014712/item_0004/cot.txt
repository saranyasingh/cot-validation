To determine how many total meters James runs per week, we need to break down the problem into smaller parts and use basic arithmetic operations.

1. **Identify the given information:**
   - Number of sprints: 3
   - Frequency of running: 3 times a week
   - Distance per sprint: 60 meters

2. **Calculate the distance covered in one session (sprint):**
   \[
   \text{Distance per session} = \text{Number of sprints} \times \text{Distance per sprint}
   \]
   Substituting the values:
   \[
   \text{Distance per session} = 3 \times 60 \text{ meters} = 180 \text{ meters}
   \]

3. **Determine the total distance covered in a week:**
   Since James runs this session three times a week, we multiply the distance per session by the frequency of running:
   \[
   \text{Total weekly distance} = \text{Distance per session} \times \text{Frequency of running}
   \]
   Substituting the values:
   \[
   \text{Total weekly distance} = 180 \text{ meters/session} \times 3 \text{ sessions/week} = 540 \text{ meters/week}
   \]

**Explanation:** We started by calculating the distance covered in one session because it represents the base unit of measurement for James's weekly activity. Then, since James repeats this session three times a week, we multiplied the distance per session by the frequency of his runs to find the total distance he covers in a week.

Therefore, the answer is: 540
