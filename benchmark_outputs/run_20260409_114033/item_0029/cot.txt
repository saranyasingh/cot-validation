To solve this problem, we need to determine how far Henry traveled between his first and second stops on his 60-mile bike trip.

Let's break down the information given into premises:
1. Total distance of the trip = 60 miles.
2. First stop = After 20 miles.
3. Second stop = 15 miles before the end of the trip (which means it ends at 45 miles).

Now let's construct our logical syllogism:

Premise 1: If Henry travels 20 miles, then he reaches the first stop.
Premise 2: If Henry travels further than 20 miles but less than 45 miles, then he reaches the second stop.
Premise 3: The total distance of the trip is 60 miles.

From Premise 1 and Premise 2, we know that:
- If he travels 20 miles, he reaches the first stop.
- If he travels more than 20 miles but less than 45 miles, he reaches the second stop.

Given that the second stop is exactly 15 miles before the end of the trip, we infer that Henry must have traveled 20 miles plus some additional distance between these stops. Let's denote the distance between the first and second stops as \( d \) miles.

Since the second stop is 15 miles before the end of the trip, which is 45 miles, the distance traveled after reaching the first stop would be:
\[ 45 - 20 = 25 \text{ miles} \]

Therefore, the distance between the first and second stops (\( d \)) is:
\[ d = 25 \text{ miles} \]

Thus, the answer is: 25.
