To determine the total distance covered by each train over the two days, we need to calculate the distance traveled on the first day and then add the distance traveled on the second day.

On the first day:
- Each train travels 80 miles westward.
- Therefore, the total distance covered by each train on the first day is \(80 \text{ miles}\).

On the second day:
- Each train travels 150 miles northward.
- Therefore, the total distance covered by each train on the second day is \(150 \text{ miles}\).

Now, let's sum up the distances for both days:

Total distance covered by each train = Distance traveled on the first day + Distance traveled on the second day

\[
\text{Total distance} = 80 \text{ miles} + 150 \text{ miles}
\]

\[
\text{Total distance} = 230 \text{ miles}
\]

Thus, the total distance covered by each train in the two days is **230 miles**.
