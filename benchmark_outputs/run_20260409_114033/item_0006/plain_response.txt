To determine how much Kylar needs to pay for 16 glasses, we first calculate the cost of each glass based on the given conditions:

1. The first glass costs $5.
2. Each subsequent glass costs 60% of the price of the previous glass.

Let's denote:
- \( C_1 \) as the cost of the first glass = $5
- \( C_2 \) as the cost of the second glass (which is 60% of \( C_1 \))
- \( C_n \) as the cost of the nth glass

Given that each subsequent glass costs 60% of the previous one:
\[ C_2 = 0.6 \times C_1 \]
\[ C_3 = 0.6 \times C_2 = 0.6^2 \times C_1 \]
\[ C_4 = 0.6 \times C_3 = 0.6^3 \times C_1 \]

We can generalize this pattern:
\[ C_{n} = 0.6^{(n-1)} \times C_1 \]

Now, let's calculate the total cost for 16 glasses:
\[ \text{Total Cost} = C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + ... + C_{16} \]

Using the formula for the sum of a geometric series:
\[ S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r} \]
where:
- \( S_n \) is the sum of the first n terms,
- \( a \) is the first term ($C_1$),
- \( r \) is the common ratio (0.6),
- \( n \) is the number of terms (16).

Plugging in the values:
\[ S_{16} = \frac{5(1 - 0.6^{16})}{1 - 0.6} \]

Calculating the value inside the parentheses and simplifying:
\[ S_{16} = \frac{5(1 - 0.6^{16})}{0.4} \]

Since \( 0.6^{16} \approx 0.007920930232582623 \):
\[ S_{16} = \frac{5(1 - 0.007920930232582623)}{0.4} \]
\[ S_{16} = \frac{5 \times 0.99207906976741738}{0.4} \]
\[ S_{16} = \frac{4.9603953488370869}{0.4} \]
\[ S_{16} = 12.400988372092717 \]

Rounding to two decimal places:
\[ S_{16} \approx 12.40 \]

Therefore, the total amount Kylar needs to pay for 16 glasses is approximately **$12.40**.
