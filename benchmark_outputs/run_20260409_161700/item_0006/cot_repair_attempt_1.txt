EXPLANATION
To determine how much Kylar needs to pay for 16 glasses, we first identify the cost structure given in the problem.

1. **First Glass**: Costs $5.
2. **Second Glass onwards**: Each subsequent glass costs 60% of the price of the preceding glass.

Let's break down the calculation step-by-step:

- For the first glass (the base case), it costs $5.
  
- The next glass (second glass) would then cost 60% of $5:
  \[
  0.60 \times 5 = 3 \text{ dollars}
  \]

- For the third glass (third glass):
  - It costs 60% of $3:
    \[
    0.60 \times 3 = 1.80 \text{ dollars}
    \]

- Continue this pattern until the sixteenth glass. We observe that the prices form an arithmetic sequence with a common ratio of 0.6.

**General Formula for nth term in an Arithmetic Sequence**:
\[
a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)d
\]
where \(a_n\) is the nth term, \(a_1\) is the first term ($5 initially), \(d\) is the common difference (0.60), and \(n\) is the position of the term in the sequence.

For the sixteenth glass (\(n=16\)):
\[
a_{16} = 5 + (16 - 1) \cdot 0.60
\]
\[
a_{16} = 5 + 15 \cdot 0.60
\]
\[
a_{16} = 5 + 9
\]
\[
a_{16} = 14 \text{ dollars}
\]

However, since the prices follow a geometric progression (each successive price is 60% of the previous one), the sixthteenth glass should actually cost 60% of $14:
\[
a_{16} = 14 \times 0.60 = 8.40 \text{ dollars}
\]

Now, summing up all these individual costs:
\[
Total = \sum_{i=1}^{16} i \cdot 0.60^i
\]

Using the properties of geometric sequences, the sum of the first n terms can be calculated as:
\[
S_n = a \frac{1-r^n}{1-r}
\]
where \(a\) is the first term ($5 initially), \(r\) is the common ratio (0.60), and \(n\) is the number of terms (16).

Substituting the values:
\[
S_{16} = 5 \frac{1-(0.6)^{16}}{1-0.6}
\]
\[
S_{16} = 5 \frac{1-0.00053}{0.4}
\]
\[
S_{16} = 5 \frac{0.99947}{0.4}
\]
\[
S_{16} = 5 \times 2.498675
\]
\[
S_{16} = 12.493375
\]

Therefore, Kylar needs to pay approximately $12.49 for 16 glasses.

The answer is: 12.49
