EXPLANATION

1. John drives for 3 hours at a speed of 60 mph.
   - Distance covered = Speed × Time = 60 mph × 3 hours = 180 miles.

2. John turns around due to forgetting something at home.
   - This means his trip now consists of two segments: back to home and reaching home again.

3. John's goal is to reach home within 4 hours total.
   - However, he spends the first 2 hours in standstill traffic, which does not contribute to covering any distance.
   - He also spends the next half-hour (0.5 hours) driving at 30 mph.
     - Distance covered in this period = Speed × Time = 30 mph × 0.5 hours = 15 miles.

4. After these initial periods, John has driven for a total of 2 + 0.5 = 2.5 hours.
   - Therefore, he still needs to cover an additional distance to make up the remaining 1.5 hours (4 hours total - 2.5 hours already spent).

5. For the remaining 1.5 hours, John drives at a speed of 80 mph.
   - Distance covered in these last 1.5 hours = Speed × Time = 80 mph × 1.5 hours = 120 miles.

6. To find out how far John is from home after completing his journey, we add up all the distances he has covered so far:
   - Total distance covered = Initial segment distance + Standstill traffic distance + Remaining distance
   - Total distance covered = 180 miles + 15 miles + 120 miles = 315 miles.

7. Since John started at home and ended up 315 miles away, he must have been 315 miles from home when he turned around.

Therefore, the answer is: 315.
