EXPLANATION

To determine how much Janet makes every day at the farmers' market, let's break down the problem step-by-step:

1. **Total number of eggs laid per day**:
   - Janet’s ducks lay 16 eggs per day.

2. **Number of eggs eaten per day**:
   - Janet eats 3 eggs for breakfast every morning.

3. **Number of eggs left for sale**:
   - Subtract the eggs eaten from the total eggs laid:
     \[ \text{Eggs left} = 16 - 3 = 13 \]

4. **Number of muffins baked per day**:
   - Janet bakes muffins for her friends every day with 4 eggs each.

5. **Total number of muffins baked per day**:
   - Divide the number of eggs left by the number of eggs needed per muffin:
     \[ \text{Muffins baked} = \frac{13}{4} = 3.25 \]
   - Since Janet can't bake a fraction of a muffin, we round up to the nearest whole number, which means she bakes 4 muffins per day.

6. **Revenue calculation**:
   - Janet sells the remaining eggs at $2 per egg.
   - Multiply the number of eggs left by the price per egg:
     \[ \text{Daily earnings from eggs} = 13 \times 2 = \$26 \]

7. **Additional revenue from muffins**:
   - Janet also sells the muffins at $2 each.
   - Multiply the number of muffins sold by the price per muffin:
     \[ \text{Daily earnings from muffins} = 4 \times 2 = \$8 \]

8. **Total daily earnings**:
   - Add the earnings from selling eggs and baking muffins:
     \[ \text{Total daily earnings} = 26 + 8 = \$34 \]

Therefore, Janet makes \( \$34 \) every day at the farmers' market.

The answer is: 34
