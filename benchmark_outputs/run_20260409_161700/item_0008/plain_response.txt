To determine how long it will take Carla to download the remaining 160 GB (since 200 - 40 = 160) after restarting, we need to calculate her download speed before and after the restart.

First, let's find out what percentage of the total download time Carla had when the restart occurred:
- The download was initially at 80%.
- She then had to restart for 20 minutes.

So, if \( t \) is the initial download time in minutes, we know that:
\[ 0.8t + 20 = t \]
Solving for \( t \):
\[ 20 = t - 0.8t \]
\[ 20 = 0.2t \]
\[ t = \frac{20}{0.2} \]
\[ t = 100 \text{ minutes} \]

Now, we calculate the actual download rate during this period:
\[ \text{Download rate} = \frac{2GB}{\text{minutes}} \times \frac{100 \text{ minutes}}{100} = 2GB/\text{minute} \]

Next, we need to calculate the remaining time required to complete the download:
- Remaining data to be downloaded: 160 GB
- Current download rate: 2 GB/min

Time needed to finish the remaining part:
\[ \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Remaining Data}}{\text{Current Download Rate}} \]
\[ \text{Time} = \frac{160 \text{ GB}}{2 \text{ GB/min}} \]
\[ \text{Time} = 80 \text{ minutes} \]

Finally, adding the time taken for the restart:
\[ \text{Total Time} = 20 \text{ minutes} + 80 \text{ minutes} \]
\[ \text{Total Time} = 100 \text{ minutes} \]

Therefore, the total time it takes Carla to download the file is **100 minutes**.
