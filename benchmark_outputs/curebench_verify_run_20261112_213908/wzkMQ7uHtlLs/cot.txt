2. **Premise 2**: Breastfeeding involves transferring substances into the infant through breastmilk. Albuterol, when administered via inhalers like Albuterol Sulfate HFA, does indeed transfer to breastmilk due to its aerosolized form.

3. **Premise 3**: The label also mentions potential side effects such as tremor, palpitations, increased heart rate, nervousness, restlessness, insomnia, irritability, sweating, dizziness, hypotension, tachycardia, dyspnea, and allergic reactions. These symptoms could potentially affect both the mother and her child.

4. **Premise 4**: The label advises against using Albuterol Sulfate HFA in patients who are pregnant, nursing, or have severe respiratory diseases because these populations may be at higher risk for adverse effects.

5. **Premise 5**: Given Premises 2-4, it would be highly unlikely for any substance to pass entirely unaltered into breastmilk, especially considering the nature of aerosol medications being inhaled directly into the lungs.

6. **Conclusion**: Considering all premises together, the most logical conclusion is that Albuterol Sulfate HFA is not recommended for use during breastfeeding due to the potential risks posed to the baby, despite its lower plasma levels compared to systemic administration.
