To determine which withdrawal symptom nicotine helps alleviate, we need to analyze the effects of nicotine on the body and how these effects might relate to symptoms commonly associated with quitting smoking.

**Step 1:** Analyze the role of nicotine in the human body.
Nicotine acts as an addictive stimulant by binding to specific receptors in the brain, particularly those related to reward and pleasure. It also affects the release of certain neurotransmitters like dopamine, which contributes to feelings of euphoria and satisfaction when used regularly.

**Step 2:** Consider the typical withdrawal symptoms experienced during cessation.
Common withdrawal symptoms include irritability, anxiety, difficulty concentrating (often referred to as "craving"), insomnia, fatigue, and headaches. These symptoms are typically linked to reduced levels of nicotine in the body, not directly to the effects of nicotine itself but rather to the absence of this stimulating substance.

**Step 3:** Examine the options provided.
- A: Nicotine craving - This aligns well with the idea that reduced nicotine leads to increased cravings, which could be considered a form of withdrawal symptom.
- B: Headache - While headache is often cited as a common withdrawal symptom, it does not specifically link to nicotine’s mechanism of action.
- C: Nausea - Similar to headache, nausea is a general symptom that may occur during various forms of withdrawal, not necessarily tied to nicotine alone.
- D: Dizziness - Dizziness is more closely associated with changes in blood pressure and fluid balance than with nicotine withdrawal.

**Step 4:** Draw a conclusion based on the analysis.
Given that nicotine withdrawal primarily manifests through cravings and other behavioral changes rather than direct physiological effects such as headache or dizziness, the most appropriate answer would be related to the actual physical sensation being alleviated by nicotine.

Therefore, the logical conclusion is that the correct answer is A: Nicotine craving.
