Let's break down this problem into a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** If Ron stands at 14 feet, then Dean is 8 feet shorter than Ron.
- This establishes that we need to find Dean’s height first before determining the depth of the water.

**Premise 2:** The depth of the water is 2 times Dean's height.
- This gives us the relationship between the water depth and Dean's height directly.

**Step-by-step reasoning:**

1. **Find Dean's height:**
   - Given: Ron stands at 14 feet.
   - According to Premise 1, if Ron is 14 feet tall, then Dean must be \(14 - 8 = 6\) feet tall.

2. **Determine the depth of the water:**
   - Given: The depth of the water is 2 times Dean's height.
   - Using the information from Step 1 (Dean is 6 feet tall), calculate the depth:
     \[
     \text{Depth} = 2 \times \text{Dean's height} = 2 \times 6 = 12 \text{ feet}
     \]

**Conclusion:**
The depth of the water is 12 feet.

Therefore, the answer is: The depth of the water is 12 feet.
