This problem involves converting a narrative into logical statements using First-Order Logic (FOL). Let's break down each step based on the given context and convert them accordingly:

### Premises
1. **After a typhoon, 13 trees in Haley's backyard died.**
   - This can be represented as: \( D_{\text{trees}}(t_1) \rightarrow T_{\text{typhoon}, t_1} \)
     Where \( D_{\text{trees}}(t_1) \) denotes that 13 trees died at time \( t_1 \).
   
2. **She had grown 3 trees initially.**
   - This can be represented as: \( G_{\text{trees}}(t_2) \rightarrow T_{\text{initial}, t_2} \)
     Where \( G_{\text{trees}}(t_2) \) denotes that she grew 3 trees at time \( t_2 \).

### Conclusion
- **Therefore, 10 more trees died in the typhoon than those that survived.**

To derive this conclusion logically, let's assume some variables:
- \( S_t \): Set of times Haley started growing trees.
- \( F_t \): Set of times Haley finished growing trees.
  
We'll denote the total number of trees that died as \( T_{\text{total}} \) and the number of trees that survived as \( S_{\text{survived}} \).

From premise 1, if Haley died 13 trees at time \( t_1 \), then \( D_{\text{trees}}(t_1) \) holds true.
From premise 2, if Haley grew 3 trees at any point before finishing, then \( G_{\text{trees}}(t_2) \) holds true.

Since the number of trees that died is greater than the number of trees that survived, we conclude that:
\[ T_{\text{total}} > S_{\text{survived}} \]

Let's summarize the conversion into FOL:

### Facts
1. \( D_{\text{trees}}(t_1) \rightarrow T_{\text{typhoon}, t_1} \)
2. \( G_{\text{trees}}(t_2) \rightarrow T_{\text{initial}, t_2} \)

### Rules
No rules needed for this simple case; just facts.

### Inferences
1. \( T_{\text{total}} > S_{\text{survived}} \)

Thus, the final output in FOL form is:

```
FACT-1: D_{\text{trees}}(t_1) \rightarrow T_{\text{typhoon}, t_1}

FACT-2: G_{\text{trees}}(t_2) \rightarrow T_{\text{initial}, t_2}

INF-1: T_{\text{total}} > S_{\text{survived}}
```
