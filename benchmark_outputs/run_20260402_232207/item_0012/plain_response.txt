Let's denote the number of apples Jackie has as \( J \).

According to the problem:
1. Adam has 4 more apples than Jackie.
   So, Adam has \( J + 4 \) apples.
2. Together, Adam and Jackie have 14 apples.
   Therefore, \( (J + 4) + J = 14 \).

Now let's solve for \( J \):
\[
(J + 4) + J = 14 \\
2J + 4 = 14 \\
2J = 10 \\
J = 5
\]

So, Jackie has 5 apples.

Next, we calculate how many apples Adam has:
\[
\text{Adam} = J + 4 = 5 + 4 = 9
\]

Now, we find out how many apples Bob has:
\[
\text{Bob} = \text{Adam} + (\text{Adam} + \text{Jackie}) = 9 + (9 + 5) = 9 + 14 = 23
\]

Therefore, Bob has 23 apples.

The answer is: 23
