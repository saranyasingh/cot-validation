Let's break down this problem into a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** There were originally 21 children on the bus.
- This sets up our initial condition and provides us with the starting point.

**Premise 2:** At the bus stop, 10 children got off the bus.
- This introduces an event that occurred before the current state, indicating movement outwards from the original number.

**Premise 3:** After some additional children got on the bus (let’s call this number Y), there were 16 children on the bus.
- This introduces another event that occurred after the first one but before reaching the final count.

We need to find the value of Y, which represents how many children got on the bus at the bus stop.

**Step-by-step Logical Reasoning:**

1. **Initial Condition:** We start with 21 children on the bus.
   - \( \text{Total Children} = 21 \)

2. **Event 1:** Some children get off the bus at the bus stop.
   - Let the number of children who got off be denoted as 10.
   - Therefore, the new total after 10 children leave would be:
     \[
     21 - 10 = 11
     \]

3. **Event 2:** After these 10 children have left, some more children get on the bus.
   - Let the number of children who get on the bus be denoted as Y.
   - So, we add these children to the remaining 11 children:
     \[
     11 + Y = 16
     \]

4. **Solving for Y:** To find the number of children who got on the bus, we solve the equation:
   \[
   11 + Y = 16
   \]
   Subtract 11 from both sides:
   \[
   Y = 16 - 11
   \]
   \[
   Y = 5
   \]

Therefore, the answer is: 5

The final answer is: 5 children got on the bus at the bus stop.
