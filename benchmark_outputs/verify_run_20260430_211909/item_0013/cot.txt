EXPLANATION

Let's break down the problem into parts and analyze them logically.

**Premise 1:** Carlos plants a lemon tree that costs $90 to plant.
- This establishes the initial investment required for the tree.

**Premise 2:** The tree grows 7 lemons per year.
- This indicates the annual production potential of the tree.

**Premise 3:** He sells each lemon for $1.50.
- This determines the revenue generated from selling lemons annually.

**Premise 4:** It costs $3 per year to water and feed the tree.
- This shows ongoing expenses associated with maintaining the tree.

Now, let's calculate how much money Carlos earns annually from selling lemons:
\[ \text{Annual Revenue} = (\text{Number of Lemons Sold}) \times (\text{Price per Lemon}) \]
\[ \text{Annual Revenue} = 7 \times \$1.50 = \$10.50 \]

Next, we need to determine the net profit after accounting for the cost of watering and feeding the tree each year:
\[ \text{Net Profit Per Year} = \text{Annual Revenue} - \text{Cost of Watering and Feeding} \]
\[ \text{Net Profit Per Year} = \$10.50 - \$3 = \$7.50 \]

Finally, to find out how many years it takes for Carlos to start earning money, we divide the total initial investment by the net profit per year:
\[ \text{Years to Start Earning Money} = \frac{\text{Total Initial Investment}}{\text{Net Profit Per Year}} \]
\[ \text{Years to Start Earning Money} = \frac{\$90}{\$7.50} = 12 \]

Therefore, it will take Carlos **12 years** before he starts earning money on the lemon tree.
