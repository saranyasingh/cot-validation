EXPLANATION

1. Let's start by defining the variables based on the information given:
   - Let \( S \) represent Suzy's iPhone age.
   - Let \( B \) represent Ben's iPhone age.
   - Let \( Br \) represent Brandon's iPhone age.

2. From the problem statement, we know that:
   - Suzy's iPhone (\( S \)) is 1 year old.
   - Ben's iPhone (\( B \)) is twice as old as Suzy's iPhone, so \( B = 2S \).
   - Brandon's iPhone (\( Br \)) is four times as old as Ben's iPhone, so \( Br = 4B \).

3. We need to find out how old Brandon's iPhone is when Suzy's iPhone is 1 year old. Since Suzy's iPhone is already known to be 1 year old, we don't need to use it further in our calculations.

4. First, calculate Ben's iPhone age using the relationship between Suzy's and Ben's iPhones:
   \[
   B = 2S = 2 \times 1 = 2 \text{ years}
   \]

5. Next, calculate Brandon's iPhone age using the relationship between Ben's and Brandon's iPhones:
   \[
   Br = 4B = 4 \times 2 = 8 \text{ years}
   \]

6. Therefore, when Suzy's iPhone was 1 year old, Brandon's iPhone was 8 years old.

THE ANSWER IS: 8
