To determine how far John is from home at the end of his trip, we need to calculate the distance covered during each segment of his journey.

1. **First Segment**: John drives for 3 hours at a speed of 60 mph.
   \[
   \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 60 \text{ mph} \times 3 \text{ hours} = 180 \text{ miles}
   \]

2. **Second Segment**: John spends the first 2 hours in standstill traffic.
   \[
   \text{Distance} = 0 \text{ miles (since he doesn't move)}
   \]
   
3. **Third Segment**: John spends the next half hour driving at a speed of 30 mph.
   \[
   \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 30 \text{ mph} \times 0.5 \text{ hours} = 15 \text{ miles}
   \]

4. **Fourth Segment**: The remaining time of the 4 hours is spent driving at an average speed of \( \frac{80 + 0}{2} = 40 \) mph.
   \[
   \text{Distance} = \text{Average Speed} \times \text{Remaining Time} = 40 \text{ mph} \times 2 \text{ hours} = 80 \text{ miles}
   \]

Now, sum up all the distances:
\[
180 \text{ miles} + 0 \text{ miles} + 15 \text{ miles} + 80 \text{ miles} = 275 \text{ miles}
\]

Therefore, the total distance John has traveled is 275 miles. To find out how far he is from home at the end of those 4 hours, we note that he started 180 miles away and drove another 275 miles, so:

\[
\text{Total Distance Traveled} - \text{Initial Distance} = 275 \text{ miles} - 180 \text{ miles} = 95 \text{ miles}
\]

Thus, John is 95 miles away from home at the end of those 4 hours. 

The answer is: 95
