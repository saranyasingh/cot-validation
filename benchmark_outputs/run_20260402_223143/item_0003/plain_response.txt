Let's break down the problem step by step:

1. Edward initially had some amount of money.
2. He spent $6 on two identical books.
3. After buying the books, he has $12 left.
4. We need to find out how much each book costs.

First, let's determine how much money Edward had before spending on the books:
- Edward has $12 left after buying the books.
- He spent $6 on books.
- Therefore, before buying the books, he must have had $12 + $6 = $18.

Now, we know that this $18 was split between two identical books. To find the cost of one book, we divide the total amount by the number of books:
\[ \text{Cost per book} = \frac{\$18}{2} = \$9 \]

So, each book cost $9.

The answer is: $9
