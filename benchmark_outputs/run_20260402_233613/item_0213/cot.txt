Let's break down this problem into a logical syllogism:

Premise 1: Together Adam and Jackie have 12 apples.
Premise 2: He (Adam) has 9 apples more than Adam and Jackie together do.
Premise 3: Adam has 8 more apples than Jackie.

From Premise 1:
- Let \( A \) represent the number of apples Adam has.
- Let \( J \) represent the number of apples Jackie has.

The first statement tells us that:
\[ A + J = 12 \]

From Premise 2:
- We know Adam has 9 apples more than what both have together.
- This means Adam’s total should be \( 12 + 9 \).

Substituting \( 12 + 9 \) for \( A \):
\[ A = 12 + 9 \]
\[ A = 21 \]

From Premise 3:
- Adam has 8 more apples than Jackie.
- So if we denote Jackie’s apples as \( J \), then:
\[ A = J + 8 \]

Now substituting \( A = 21 \) into the equation:
\[ 21 = J + 8 \]

Solving for \( J \):
\[ J = 21 - 8 \]
\[ J = 13 \]

Therefore, Jackie has 13 apples.

To summarize our steps logically:
1. Combined they have 12 apples.
2. Adam has 9 more than combined.
3. Adam has 8 more than Jackie.
4. From these premises, we deduced Adam had 21 apples.
5. Using this information about Adam, we found out Jackie had 13 apples.

Thus, the answer is: He (Adam) has 21 apples.
