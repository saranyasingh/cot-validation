To determine how many cakes the baker has left after selling some and buying more, we need to follow these steps:

### Step-by-Step Logical Reasoning

#### Premise 1:
The baker initially had 121 cakes.
- This is our starting point and is clearly stated.

#### Premise 2:
The baker sold 105 cakes.
- We know this because it's explicitly mentioned that he sold 105 cakes.

#### Premise 3:
After selling 105 cakes, the number of remaining cakes is calculated as:
\[ \text{Remaining cakes} = \text{Initial cakes} - \text{Cakes sold} \]
\[ \text{Remaining cakes} = 121 - 105 \]

Let's perform the subtraction:
\[ 121 - 105 = 16 \]

So, after selling 105 cakes, the baker still has 16 cakes.

#### Premise 4:
Then, the baker bought 170 new cakes.
- This information directly follows from the previous action since he was given that he "bought 170 new cakes."

#### Conclusion:
To find out how many cakes the baker now has, we add the 170 new cakes to the 16 remaining cakes:
\[ \text{Total cakes now} = \text{Remaining cakes} + \text{New cakes bought} \]
\[ \text{Total cakes now} = 16 + 170 \]

Perform the addition:
\[ 16 + 170 = 186 \]

Therefore, the baker currently has 186 cakes.

**Final Answer:** 
The baker still has **186 cakes**.
