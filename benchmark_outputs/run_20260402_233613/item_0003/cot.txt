To determine the cost of each book, we need to break down the information given and apply it logically:

**Premise 1:** Edward spent $6 on two books.
- This tells us that the total expenditure was $6 and these expenditures were made on two items (books).

**Premise 2:** Each book costs the same amount of money.
- This suggests that all books have the same price.

**Premise 3:** Edward now has $12 left after buying the books.
- This implies that before spending the $6, Edward had more than $12 because he ended up with exactly $12 after the purchase.

Let's denote:
- \( x \) as the cost per book.

Since Edward bought two books at a total cost of $6, we can write this relationship as:
\[ 2x = 6 \]

Now, solving for \( x \):
\[ x = \frac{6}{2} \]
\[ x = 3 \]

Therefore, each book cost **$3**.
