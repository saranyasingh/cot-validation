Let's break down this problem into a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** Baker made 170 cakes.
- This establishes the initial quantity of cakes available to him.

**Premise 2:** Baker sold 78 cakes.
- This indicates the number of cakes that were no longer part of his inventory after some were sold.

**Premise 3:** Baker bought 31 new cakes.
- This shows an addition to his stock, indicating the increase in cake count due to purchases.

**Conclusion:** We need to determine how many more cakes the baker sold than those he bought.
- To find out if there was any surplus or deficit between what was sold and what was bought, we compare these two quantities directly.

**Logical Syllogism:**
1. (P1) Baker made 170 cakes.
   - Premise: Initial stock.
2. (P2) Baker sold 78 cakes.
   - Premise: Number of cakes sold.
3. (P3) Baker bought 31 new cakes.
   - Premise: New additions to stock.
4. (C) We want to know how many more cakes were sold than bought.
5. From P2 and P3:
   - If 78 cakes were sold and 31 cakes were bought,
   - Then 78 > 31.
6. Therefore, it logically follows that the baker sold more cakes than he bought.

**The answer is: X = More**

This conclusion is drawn based on direct comparison of the quantities mentioned in premises 2 and 3, which show that the number of cakes sold exceeded the number of cakes bought.
