Let's break down this problem using a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** Jack received 9 emails in the morning.
- This establishes the number of emails he received during that specific time period.

**Premise 2:** Jack received 10 emails in the afternoon.
- This provides another piece of information about his email activity.

**Premise 3:** Jack received 7 emails in the evening.
- This gives us yet another count of emails received at a different time.

Now, to find out how many more emails Jack received in the morning compared to the evening, we need to subtract the number of emails he received in the evening from the number of emails he received in the morning.

The calculation would be:
\[ \text{Difference} = \text{Morning Emails} - \text{Evening Emails} \]
\[ \text{Difference} = 9 - 7 \]

This subtraction shows us how much larger the morning count is relative to the evening count.

Therefore, the difference is:
\[ \text{Difference} = 2 \]

So, Jack received **2 more emails in the morning than in the evening.**

The answer is: 2
