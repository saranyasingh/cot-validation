Let's break down this problem using a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** Adam could fit 10 action figures on each shelf.
- This tells us that each shelf holds exactly 10 action figures.

**Premise 2:** His room could hold 8 action figures.
- This means the total capacity of the room is 8 action figures.

To find out how many total shelves his room had, we need to determine how many sets of 10 action figures (each set corresponding to one shelf) fit into the total capacity of 8 action figures.

**Step-by-step logical reasoning:**

1. **Determine the number of complete sets of 10 action figures in the room:**
   - Each set consists of 10 action figures.
   - We divide the total number of action figures by the number of action figures per shelf:
     \[
     \frac{8}{10} = 0.8
     \]
   - Since we cannot have a fraction of a shelf, we recognize that only whole sets count towards the actual number of shelves. 

2. **Interpret the result:**
   - Having a quotient of 0.8 means there are not enough individual action figures to fill up any additional shelves beyond those already counted.
   - Therefore, the room must have 0 full shelves and no partial shelves, as even if he tried to put more than what was available, it would still be less than 8 action figures.

3. **Conclusion:**
   - Given the constraints provided, the room could only have 0 shelves, since having a fractional or non-whole number of shelves doesn't make sense in practical terms.

**Answer:** The answer is: 0

This conclusion directly follows from the given premises and our interpretation of them.
