**Premise 1:** Edward spent $16 on books.
- This is given directly as part of the information provided.

**Premise 2:** Edward spent $8 on pens.
- This is also given directly as part of the information provided.

**Premise 3:** Edward now has $19 left after his purchases.
- This statement tells us how much money Edward had before making any purchases and what he has left after spending.

**Premise 4:** To find out how much more Edward spent on books than pens, we need to calculate the difference between the amount spent on books and the amount spent on pens.
- This is derived from the fact that the total expenditure is the sum of the amounts spent on books and pens.

**Conclusion:** 
Let's denote:
- \( B \) = Amount spent on books
- \( P \) = Amount spent on pens

From Premises 1 and 2, we know:
\[ B = \$16 \]
\[ P = \$8 \]

The difference between the amount spent on books and pens is:
\[ B - P = \$16 - \$8 = \$8 \]

Therefore, Edward spent $8 more on books than on pens.

**Answer:** The answer is: 8
