Here is the conversion of the chain-of-thought reasoning into structured First-Order Logic (FOL):

### FACTS
```plaintext
FACT-1: Edward spent $16 on books :: BooksSpent(Edward, 16)
```

```plaintext
FACT-2: Edward spent $8 on pens :: PensSpent(Edward, 8)
```

### RULES
```plaintext
RULE-1: Total expenditure equals amount spent on books plus amount spent on pens :: Expenditure(Edward) = BooksSpent(Edward) + PensSpent(Edward)
```

### INFERENCES
```plaintext
INF-1: Difference between the amount spent on books and pens :: BooksSpent(Edward) - PensSpent(Edward) = BooksSpent(Edward) - PensSpent(Edward)
```

Now, let's derive the conclusion step-by-step using these facts and rules:

1. **Total expenditure calculation:**
   According to Rule-1, the total amount spent by Edward can be expressed as the sum of the amounts spent on books and pens.
   
   ```plaintext
   Expenditure(Edward) = BooksSpent(Edward) + PensSpent(Edward)
   ```

2. **Difference between book and pen expenditures:**
   We need to find the difference between the amount spent on books (\$16) and the amount spent on pens (\$8).
   
   ```plaintext
   BooksSpent(Edward) - PensSpent(Edward) = 16 - 8
   ```

3. **Final result:**
   Simplifying the above expression gives us the difference.
   
   ```plaintext
   BooksSpent(Edward) - PensSpent(Edward) = 8
   ```

Thus, Edward spent $8 more on books than on pens.

### Answer:
The answer is: 8
