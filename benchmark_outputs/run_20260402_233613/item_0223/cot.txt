To determine how many more students suggested mashed potatoes than those that suggested bacon, we need to follow these steps:

**Premise 1:** 257 students suggested adding mashed potatoes.
**Premise 2:** 120 students suggested adding bacon to the menu.
**Premise 3:** 97 students suggested adding tomatoes.

We want to find out how many more students suggested mashed potatoes than those that suggested bacon.

**Step-by-Step Logical Reasoning:**

1. **Identify the total number of students who suggested mashed potatoes**: This is given as 257 students.
2. **Identify the total number of students who suggested bacon**: This is given as 120 students.
3. **Calculate the difference between the two groups**: To do this, subtract the number of students who suggested bacon from the number of students who suggested mashed potatoes.

\[ \text{Difference} = \text{Number of students suggesting mashed potatoes} - \text{Number of students suggesting bacon} \]

\[ \text{Difference} = 257 - 120 \]

4. **Perform the subtraction**: 

\[ 257 - 120 = 137 \]

Therefore, 137 more students suggested mashed potatoes than those that suggested bacon.

The answer is: 137
