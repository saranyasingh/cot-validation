1. Each t-shirt is normally priced at $51.
2. A discount of $8 was applied to each t-shirt during the event.
3. They sold 130 t-shirts during this event.

### Rules (general principles):
1. If an item has a normal price \( P \), then after applying a discount \( D \), the new price becomes \( P - D \).
2. Given the number of items sold \( N \), if each item costs \( P - D \), then the total revenue \( R \) is calculated as \( R = (P - D) \times N \).

### Inferences (derived conclusions):
From Premises 1 and 2, we can infer:
- Normal price of each t-shirt: \( 51 \) dollars.
- Discount applied to each t-shirt: \( 8 \) dollars.

Using these facts, we can calculate the discounted price:
- Discounted price per t-shirt: \( 51 - 8 = 43 \) dollars.

Next, using the number of t-shirts sold:
- Number of t-shirts sold: \( 130 \).

Finally, calculating the total revenue:
- Total revenue: \( 43 \times 130 = 5590 \) dollars.
