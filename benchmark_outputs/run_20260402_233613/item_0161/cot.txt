**Premise 1:** Baker made 133 cakes.
- This establishes the total number of cakes baked by the baker.

**Premise 2:** Baker sold 51 cakes.
- This tells us how many cakes were sold by the baker.

**Premise 3:** To find out how many more cakes the baker made than those he sold, we need to subtract the number of cakes sold from the total number of cakes made.
- Let's denote:
  - \( T \) as the total number of cakes made,
  - \( S \) as the number of cakes sold.

**Step-by-step Logical Reasoning:**

1. **Total Cakes Made (T):**
   - According to Premise 1, the baker made 133 cakes.
   - Therefore, \( T = 133 \).

2. **Cakes Sold (S):**
   - According to Premise 2, the baker sold 51 cakes.
   - Therefore, \( S = 51 \).

3. **Difference Between Total and Sold Cakes:**
   - We need to calculate \( T - S \).
   - Substitute the values of \( T \) and \( S \):
     \[
     T - S = 133 - 51
     \]

4. **Perform the Subtraction:**
   - Subtract 51 from 133:
     \[
     133 - 51 = 82
     \]

**Conclusion:**
The baker made 82 more cakes than those he sold.

**Answer:**
The answer is: 82.
