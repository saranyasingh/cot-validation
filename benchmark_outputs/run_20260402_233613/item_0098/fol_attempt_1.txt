Let's convert the given chain-of-thought reasoning into structured First-Order Logic (FOL). We'll identify facts, rules, and inferences based on the provided information.

### Facts
These are direct statements about the problem without any quantification.

#### Fact-1: Total cakes made = 126
\[
C_{cakes} = 126
\]

#### Fact-2: Total pastries made = 48
\[
P_{pastries} = 48
\]

### Rules
These are universally quantified implications that represent external or linguistic knowledge.

#### Rule-1: If cakes are sold, then the remaining cakes are equal to the total cakes minus the cakes sold.
\[
\forall x (\text{CakeSold}(x) \rightarrow \text{RemainingCakes}(x) = C_{cakes})
\]

#### Rule-2: If pastries are sold, then the remaining pastries are equal to the total pastries minus the pastries sold.
\[
\forall y (\text{PastrySold}(y) \rightarrow \text{RemainingPastries}(y) = P_{pastries})
\]

### Inferences
These are derived conclusions based on the facts and rules.

#### Inference-1: Remaining cakes after selling 115 cakes.
\[
\text{RemainingCakes}(115) = C_{cakes} - 115
\]

Since \(C_{cakes} = 126\) and \(115 < 126\):

\[
\text{RemainingCakes}(115) = 126 - 115 = 11

\[
\text{RemainingPastries}(81) = P_{pastries} - 81
\]

Since \(P_{pastries} = 48\) and \(81 > 48\):

\[
\text{RemainingPastries}(81) = 48 - 81 = -33
\]
