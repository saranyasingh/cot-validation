Let's break down this problem using a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** Paul started with 2 books.
- This establishes the initial number of books Paul had before any sales or purchases.

**Premise 2:** After selling some books and buying 150 new ones, Paul now has 58 books.
- We know the current total number of books (58) after these transactions.

To find out how many books Paul sold, we need to determine what happened between his starting point and his current count. 

**Step-by-step Reasoning:**

1. **Initial Number of Books**: Let \( B_0 \) be the initial number of books Paul had. From Premise 1, we have:
   \[
   B_0 = 2
   \]

2. **Books Bought**: Let \( B_{\text{new}} \) be the number of new books Paul bought. According to Premise 2, Paul bought 150 new books:
   \[
   B_{\text{new}} = 150
   \]

3. **Total Books After Purchase**: The total number of books after purchasing new ones should equal the sum of the initial books plus the new books:
   \[
   B_0 + B_{\text{new}} = \text{Total Books}
   \]
   Substituting the known values:
   \[
   2 + 150 = \text{Total Books}
   \]
   Therefore,
   \[
   \text{Total Books} = 152
   \]

4. **Books Sold Calculation**: To find out how many books were sold, subtract the initial number of books from the total number of books after all transactions:
   \[
   \text{Books Sold} = \text{Total Books} - B_0
   \]
   Substituting the values we calculated:
   \[
   \text{Books Sold} = 152 - 2
   \]
   Therefore,
   \[
   \text{Books Sold} = 150
   \]

Thus, Paul sold 150 books.

The answer is: **150**
