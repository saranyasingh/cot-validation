To convert this chain-of-thought reasoning into structured first-order logic (FOL), we will follow these steps:

### Facts
These are the given premises without any quantification or rules.

#### Fact-1: Matthew gave equal numbers of crackers and cakes to his 4 friends.
- Let \( x \) represent the number of crackers each friend received.
- Since Matthew distributed crackers equally among his 4 friends, the total number of crackers can be represented as \( 4x \).
- Similarly, let \( y \) represent the number of cakes each friend received.
- Since Matthew distributed cakes equally among his 4 friends, the total number of cakes can be represented as \( 4y \).

Thus, the facts are:
Fact-1: CrackerTotal(x, 4) :: x * 4
Fact-2: CakeTotal(y, 4) :: y * 4

### Rules
These are derived rules based on the logical structure provided in the premise.

#### Rule-1: Equal Distribution of Crackers and Cakes
- For every friend, the number of crackers (\( x \)) must be equal to the number of cakes (\( y \)).
- Mathematically, this can be expressed as:
  \[
  x = y
  \]

### Inferences
These are logically derived conclusions based on the premises and rules.

#### Infer-1: Number of Crackers per Friend
- Given that the total number of crackers is 32 and they are divided equally among 4 friends, we calculate:
  \[
  x = \frac{32}{4}
  \]
- Simplifying this gives us:
  \[
  x = 8
  \]

Thus, the answer is:
Infer-1: NumberCrackersPerFriend :: 8
