1. **Premise 1:** Matthew initially has 29 crackers and 30 cakes.
   - This establishes the total number of items Matthew has before any are distributed.

2. **Premise 2:** Matthew wants to distribute these items equally among his 2 friends.
   - This introduces the idea that the distribution must be fair and equal.

3. **Premise 3:** Since there are 2 friends, we need to divide both the crackers and cakes into two equal parts.
   - Dividing the crackers:
     \[
     \frac{29}{2} = 14.5
     \]
     Since it's not possible to split a whole number of crackers exactly in half (because 29 isn't an even number), this indicates there might be some discrepancy or misinterpretation in the problem setup.

4. **Premise 4:** Similarly, dividing the cakes:
     \[
     \frac{30}{2} = 15
     \]
     There seems to be no issue here; 30 divided by 2 equals 15, which is a whole number, so no further division is necessary.

5. **Conclusion:** Each friend gets 15 cakes because they were given equal shares of both crackers and cakes.
