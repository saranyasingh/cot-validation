To determine how many tomatoes the farmer has left after yesterday's picking, we need to follow these steps:

**Premise 1:** The initial number of tomatoes in the garden is 160.
- **Reasoning:** This sets the starting point for our calculation.

**Premise 2:** The farmer picked 56 tomatoes yesterday.
- **Reasoning:** This tells us what happened before yesterday.

**Premise 3:** After picking 56 tomatoes yesterday, the remaining number of tomatoes is calculated as:
\[ \text{Remaining tomatoes} = \text{Initial number of tomatoes} - \text{Tomatoes picked yesterday} \]
- **Reasoning:** We subtract the number of tomatoes picked from the total to find out how many are left.

Now let's apply this logic step-by-step:

**Step 1:** Start with the initial number of tomatoes.
\[ \text{Initial number of tomatoes} = 160 \]

**Step 2:** Subtract the number of tomatoes picked yesterday.
\[ \text{Remaining tomatoes after yesterday} = 160 - 56 \]

Let's do the subtraction:
\[ 160 - 56 = 104 \]

So, after yesterday's picking, the farmer has 104 tomatoes left.

The answer is: 104
