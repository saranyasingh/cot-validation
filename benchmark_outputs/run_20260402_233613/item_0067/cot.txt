Let's break down the problem into steps using a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** Rachel completed 5 pages of math homework.
- This establishes the number of pages in the first category (math).

**Premise 2:** Rachel completed 10 pages of reading homework.
- This introduces another set of pages (reading) that we need to consider separately.

**Premise 3:** Rachel completed 6 more pages of biology homework.
- This adds an additional amount to the total count of pages in the third category (biology).

**Conclusion:** To find the total number of pages Rachel had to complete across all categories, we add up the pages from each subject:
- Math: 5 pages
- Reading: 10 pages
- Biology: 6 pages

**Logical Syllogism:**
1. **Math Homework:** Rachel has completed 5 pages.
   - Premise 1: "Rachel had to complete 5 pages of math homework."
   
2. **Reading Homework:** Rachel has also completed 10 pages.
   - Premise 2: "Rachel completed 10 pages of reading homework."

3. **Biology Homework:** Adding 6 more pages to the reading homework,
   - Premise 3: "Rachel completed 6 more pages of biology homework."

4. **Total Pages:** Summing the pages from each category,
   - Conclusion: "To find the total number of pages Rachel had to complete across all categories, we add up the pages from each subject," which gives us \(5 + 10 + 6 = 21\) pages.

Therefore, the total number of pages Rachel had to complete is 21.
