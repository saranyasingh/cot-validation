Premise 1: Mary needs to complete her cake baking.
Premise 2: The recipe requires 12 cups of sugar.
Premise 3: Mary has already added 10 cups of sugar.
Premise 4: The recipe requires 14 cups of flour.
Premise 5: Mary hasn't yet added any flour to the mixture.

Logical Syllogism:
- From Premise 1, we deduce that Mary's task involves completing the cake baking process.
- From Premise 2, we know the total amount of sugar needed for the recipe.
- From Premise 3, we learn how much sugar Mary has already added.
- From Premise 4, we find out what the required quantity of flour is.
- From Premise 5, we realize that no flour has been added yet.

Conclusion: To determine how many more cups of flour than sugar Mary still needs to add:

Step 1: Calculate the remaining sugar needed.
Remaining Sugar = Total Required Sugar - Already Added Sugar
Remaining Sugar = 12 cups (total) - 10 cups (already added)
Remaining Sugar = 2 cups

Step 2: Determine the difference between the remaining sugar and flour needed.
Difference = Remaining Sugar - Flour Needed
Difference = 2 cups (remaining sugar) - 14 cups (flour needed)
