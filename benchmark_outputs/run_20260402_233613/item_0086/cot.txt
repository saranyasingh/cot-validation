To determine how many bananas are in each group, we need to divide the total number of bananas by the number of groups they are organized into. Here’s the logical reasoning:

1. **Total number of bananas** = 203
2. **Number of groups of bananas** = 7

We want to find out how many bananas are in each group when these 203 bananas are divided among 7 groups. This can be expressed as:
\[ \text{Bananas per group} = \frac{\text{Total number of bananas}}{\text{Number of groups of bananas}} \]

Substituting the given values:
\[ \text{Bananas per group} = \frac{203}{7} \]

Now, let's perform this division step-by-step:

- First, 7 goes into 20 twice (since \(7 \times 2 = 14\)), so we write down 2.
- Subtracting 14 from 20 gives us 6.
- Bring down the next digit, which is 3, making it 63.
- Now, 7 goes into 63 nine times (since \(7 \times 9 = 63\)), so we write down 9.
- Subtracting 63 from 63 leaves no remainder.

So, the division results in:
\[ \frac{203}{7} = 29 \]

Therefore, each group of bananas contains 29 bananas.

The answer is: Each group of bananas has 29 bananas.
