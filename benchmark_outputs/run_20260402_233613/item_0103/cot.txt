Let's break down the problem into logical steps using a syllogistic approach:

**Premise 1:** Initially, there were 5 roses and 3 orchids in the vase.
- This establishes the initial conditions before any changes occurred.

**Premise 2:** Jessica cut some more flowers (roses and/or orchids) from her flower garden.
- This introduces an action that led to the subsequent state.

**Premise 3:** After cutting, there are now 12 roses and 2 orchids in the vase.
- This directly states the current condition after the cuts.

**Premise 4:** To find out how many more roses than orchids there are now, we need to subtract the number of orchids from the number of roses.
- We use this calculation to determine the difference between the two types of flowers present.

**Logical Syllogism:**
1. **Initial Condition**: There are 5 roses + 3 orchids = 8 flowers in total initially.
2. **Action Taken**: Jessica added some more flowers (either all roses or both).
3. **Final Condition**: Now, there are 12 roses - x orchids = 2 orchids (where x is the unknown number of additional flowers).

To solve:
\[ \text{Number of roses} - \text{Number of orchids} = 0 \]
Since there are no other flowers mentioned, the only way to achieve 12 roses and 2 orchids is if Jessica added exactly 10 roses and removed 8 orchids.

Therefore, Jessica must have cut 8 orchids and planted 10 roses.

**Conclusion:**
The number of roses minus the number of orchids is:
\[ 10 \text{ roses} - 8 \text{ orchids} = 2 \]

Thus, the answer is: 2.
