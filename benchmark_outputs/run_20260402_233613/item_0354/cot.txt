To determine how many ripe apples the tree had initially, we need to analyze the information given:

1. Rachel picked 2 ripe apples.
2. After picking, there are now 7 apples left on the tree.
3. Out of these remaining 7 apples, 6 are ripe.

Now let's break down the problem logically:

**Premise 1:** Rachel picked 2 ripe apples.
- This means that before she picked them, there were more than 2 ripe apples on the tree because if Rachel hadn't picked any, the number would remain unchanged or decrease (since no new apples could be added).

**Premise 2:** After Rachel picked 2 ripe apples, there are 7 apples left on the tree.
- Since the total number of apples remained 7 after Rachel picked some, this implies that none of the apples Rachel picked were part of the original count of ripe apples.

**Premise 3:** Among the remaining 7 apples, 6 are ripe.
- This directly tells us that out of the 7 apples currently on the tree, 6 are ripe.

Given Premises 1 and 3:
- We know that Rachel picked 2 ripe apples but they weren’t originally part of the 7 apples that are still on the tree.
- Therefore, these 2 apples must have been among the initial 7 apples that were present before Rachel picked them.

Since all 6 of the ripe apples that are still on the tree came from the original batch of 7 apples, and 4 of these original apples were not picked by Rachel, the remaining ripe apples must have come from the other 3 apples in the original group.

Thus, the number of ripe apples the tree had initially was 4.

The answer is: **4**
