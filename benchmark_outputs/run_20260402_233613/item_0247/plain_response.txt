Let's break down the problem step by step:

1. Initially, there were 28 children on the bus.
2. Some number of children (let's call this x) got off the bus at the bus stop.
3. After these children got off, 82 new children boarded the bus.
4. The total number of children on the bus after all these changes was 30.

We can set up an equation to represent this situation:
\[ 28 - x + 82 = 30 \]

Now, let's solve for \(x\):
\[ 28 + 82 - x = 30 \]
\[ 110 - x = 30 \]
\[ x = 110 - 30 \]
\[ x = 80 \]

So, 80 children got off the bus.

To find out how many more children got on the bus than those that got off, we subtract the number of children who got off from the number of children who got on:
\[ 82 - 80 = 2 \]

Therefore, 2 more children got on the bus than those that got off.

The answer is: 2
