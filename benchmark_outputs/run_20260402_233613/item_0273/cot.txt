Premise 1: The depth of the water is 15 times Dean's height.
Premise 2: Dean is 4 feet taller than Ron.
Premise 3: Ron stands at 13 feet.

Let's break down the logical reasoning:

**Step 1:** Determine Dean's height.
- From Premise 2, we know that Dean is 4 feet taller than Ron.
- Given that Ron stands at 13 feet, we can calculate Dean's height as:
  \[
  \text{Dean's height} = \text{Ron's height} + 4 \text{ feet} = 13 \text{ feet} + 4 \text{ feet} = 17 \text{ feet}
  \]

**Step 2:** Calculate the depth of the water based on Dean's height.
- According to Premise 1, the depth of the water is 15 times Dean's height.
- Substituting Dean's height into this equation gives us:
  \[
  \text{Depth of the water} = 15 \times \text{Dean's height} = 15 \times 17 \text{ feet}
  \]
  
**Step 3:** Perform the multiplication to find the exact depth.
\[
15 \times 17 = 255 \text{ feet}
\]

**Conclusion:** The depth of the water is 255 feet.

The answer is: 255
