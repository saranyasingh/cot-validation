To determine how much Janet makes every day at the farmers' market, we need to follow these steps:

1. Calculate the total number of eggs laid by Janes's ducks.
2. Determine the number of eggs eaten for breakfast.
3. Subtract the number of eggs eaten for breakfast from the total to find out how many eggs are left.
4. Multiply the remaining number of eggs by the price per egg to get the daily earnings.

Let's break this down into a logical syllogism:

**Premise 1:** Janet's ducks lay 16 eggs per day.
- This is our starting point, stating that the ducks produce a certain amount of eggs each day.

**Premise 2:** Janet eats three eggs for breakfast every morning.
- We know the exact quantity of eggs consumed for breakfast.

**Premise 3:** Janet bakes muffins for her friends using four eggs daily.
- This tells us another specific use of the eggs, but it doesn't affect the calculation directly.

**Premise 4:** Janet sells the rest of the eggs at the farmers' market for $2 per egg.
- This gives us the value of the eggs sold at the market.

**Step-by-step reasoning:**

1. Total eggs laid per day = 16
2. Eggs eaten for breakfast = 3
3. Remaining eggs after breakfast = 16 - 3 = 13
4. Eggs used for baking muffins = 4
5. Remaining eggs for sale = 13 - 4 = 9
6. Earnings from selling eggs = 9 * $2 = $18

Therefore, Janet makes $18 every day at the farmers' market.

The answer is: 18
