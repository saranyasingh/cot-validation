Let's break down this problem using a logical syllogism:

1. **Premise 1:** "Dave had won some tickets."
   - This is our starting point and it introduces that Dave started with an unspecified number of tickets.

2. **Premise 2:** "He used 12 tickets to buy some toys."
   - This tells us how much money or resources Dave spent on his purchases (in this case, buying toys).

3. **Premise 3:** "If he still has 14 tickets left."
   - This provides information about what remains after the purchase was made. It implies there were more tickets initially than were used up.

Now let's combine these premises into a logical syllogism:

**Logical Syllogism:**

- Premise 1: Dave had won some tickets.
- Premise 2: He used 12 tickets to buy some toys.
- Conclusion: Therefore, if he still has 14 tickets left, then he must have won \(14 + 12\) tickets initially.

To find out exactly how many tickets Dave won:
\[ \text{Initial Tickets} = \text{Tickets Used} + \text{Remaining Tickets} \]
\[ \text{Initial Tickets} = 12 + 14 \]
\[ \text{Initial Tickets} = 26 \]

Therefore, the answer is: 26.
