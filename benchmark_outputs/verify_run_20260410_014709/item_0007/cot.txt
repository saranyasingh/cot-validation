EXPLANATION

1. We start with the information given about the relationship between the number of sheep in different cities.
   - Step 1a: "Toulouse has twice as many sheep as Charleston."

         \( \text{Sheep in Charleston} = 4 \times \text{Sheep in Seattle} \)
         \( \text{Sheep in Charleston} = 4 \times 20 = 80 \)

3. Now, using the first piece of information (Toulouse has twice as many sheep as Charleston), we calculate the number of sheep in Toulouse.
   - Step 1c: "Toulouse has twice as many sheep as Charleston."
      Since Charleston has 80 sheep,
         \( \text{Sheep in Toulouse} = 2 \times \text{Sheep in Charleston} \)
         \( \text{Sheep in Toulouse} = 2 \times 80 = 160 \)

4. Finally, to find the total number of sheep across all three cities, we sum up the numbers obtained so far.
   - Step 1d: "How many sheep do Toulouse, Charleston, and Seattle have together?"
      Adding the sheep counts:
         \( \text{Total Sheep} = \text{Sheep in Toulouse} + \text{Sheep in Charleston} + \text{Sheep in Seattle} \)
         \( \text{Total Sheep} = 160 + 80 + 20 = 260 \)
